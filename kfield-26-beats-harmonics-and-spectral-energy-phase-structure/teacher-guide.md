# Teacher guide

This is a complete topical course for high-school seniors: 17 core periods and three capstone periods, each 55 minutes. Assumed entry capabilities are signed arithmetic, fractions, ratios, powers, solving linear equations and reading coordinate graphs. Courses 12,14,25 provide prior context; every operation needed here is rebuilt locally, including radians, dot products, complex arithmetic and matrix action. No calculus or university linear algebra is required.

For each core period use approximately 5 minutes retrieving earlier capabilities, 20 minutes explanation and a worked operation, 15 minutes guided practice, and 15 minutes independent diagnostics and repair. These are allocations, not empirical timing claims. Divide multi-period lessons at the listed topics. Do not attempt all of Lesson 5 in one period. Add a recovery period when a learner cannot reproduce the named operation; record the added period and keep the next dependency pending.

Begin with a blank circle and four coordinate points. Have students physically trace the rotation before using symbols. For complex multiplication, use one quarter-turn at a time and require students to replace i² by −1. In the DFT, cover the compact formula initially and calculate directly from the four multiplier rows. Uncover it when students can explain every row.

## Diagnostic response guide

If students confuse Hz with angular frequency, ask them to count complete turns and then radians in one second. If they call 1 Hz the beat frequency of 5/7 Hz, have them draw both positive and negative lobes of the signed factor and fold the negative lobe upward. If they lose direction in Lesson 3, compare sine values at the same time. If the DFT inverse is four times too large, have them check the weight of the constant root pattern.

When a learner divides by zero, require a status entry and the original inputs, then ask which intermediate values remain defined. When students replace a negative gain with its magnitude, have them reconstruct the four counts and identify the changed coordinate. A zero even-comparator response should prompt inspection of the odd component, not deletion of it.

For source reading, distinguish statements about byte identity, reported observations, arithmetic replay, source-proposed mechanisms and physical transfer. Teach the six representations as two works. Have students count the source ledger themselves. Shared cost levels do not justify deleting within-family responses or claiming fourteen independent cost inputs.

## Assessment and access

The capstone repeats previously practiced operations in a single complete packet. A four-point row rubric allocates input/domain, operation, reconstruction/classification and retained-information/boundary points. Give written correction opportunities: a learner should fix the earliest incorrect input or rule and then rerun affected dependent steps. Do not reward numerical answers that silently drop signs, phases, nulls or residuals.

All source values required for core tasks are printed in the lessons/capstone. Teachers do not need access to the private archive to conduct class. Exact references remain in sources.md for authorized audit. A scientific calculator is optional for source-card decimal division; accept honest hand-rounding with its stated precision. Finite synthetic arithmetic is exact and requires no code. Code in verify_examples.py supplies an independently replayable teacher check, not a prerequisite for students.
