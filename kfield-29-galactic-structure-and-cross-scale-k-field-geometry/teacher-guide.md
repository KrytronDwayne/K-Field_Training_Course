# Teacher guide

Teach this as a course in constructing and testing typed cross-domain comparisons. Its scientific subject is the supplied K-Field/galaxy archive. Its finite mathematics is developed from first principles. Source reports are provisionally used within their stated contracts, while fresh calculations, interpretations and proposed mechanisms remain distinguishable. Students should be able to say both what a calculation establishes and what information a next experiment needs.

## Preparation and entry check

Give three quick questions: calculate 3/4-1/2; solve 2t=5/2; locate (-1,2) on a graph. Answers are 1/4,5/4 and one left/two up. If these are unstable, add a recovery period on common denominators, inverse operations and signed coordinates before lesson 01. This is a prerequisite repair, not a silent reduction in later coverage. No prior calculus, matrix algebra or quantum mechanics is assumed. Earlier course IDs refer to the series; the required mathematical bridges are present here.

Provide each student the lesson, terminology page, source-comparison ledger and capstone task section. Keep the answer sections available for feedback after an attempt. Full catalog access and source-array replay are not student requirements. All core numeric tasks can be solved with pencil and paper; the Python verification script is an author/teacher check, not a hidden prerequisite.

## Pacing and activities

Use each 55-minute period as approximately 5 minutes retrieval, 20 explanation/worked example, 15 guided practice and 15 independent diagnostic/repair. The allocations are planning guides rather than a requirement to advance when understanding is absent.

|---|---|---|
| 01, one period | arrows, unit sphere, axis comparison | signed direction card; repair lost antipodal sign |
| 02, two | response vs address; domain/parity | two separately typed maps; repair division by zero or cycles/radians mixing |
| 03, two | basis/center reconstruction; units/origin/ownership | reversible center ledger; repair phi=A phi or inverse-metric confusion |
| 04, two | half-step closure; source comparison | two root/normal calculations; repair shared identity→same physical normal |
| 05, three | positive metric/inverse; skew counterexample; ellipse and domain repair | signed residual comparison; repair rounding→nearest claim |
| 06, two | enumerate all states; parent-child/residual ledger | complete labeled count tree; repair sparse-bin deletion |
| 07, two | freeze contract; source and toy differences | preserved old/new branch record; repair post-fit holdout claim |
| 08, two | normalized transforms; future test | fixed prediction with opposing outcome; repair post hoc similarity claim |

For unit I (after L02), ask students to distinguish a directed observation, an even response and an unoriented axis. For unit II (after L05), require reconstruction from address and residual and an explanation of why nearest-center assignment is a separate problem from ownership. For unit III (after L08), require all seven signs from the source ledger, both toy phase branches and a future outcome that contradicts the proposed direction of effect.

These cumulative checks also audit terminology: q_resp versus plane q_j versus metric address q; response u versus ownership u; full-step coordinate versus half-step state label; inverse basis versus inverse metric; geometric availability versus empirical occupancy. Let students use the terminology sheet, then ask them to explain the type in ordinary words.

## Teaching the source boundaries

The galaxy cell is derived from sky positions, with a different earlier axis pathway. Its lengths remain unit-sphere reconstruction quantities. Mature Cell3 geometry comes from S02, with intentionally negative determinant and an explicit affine origin. Historical G02/G03 values remain historical; do not use their carrier numbers to overwrite the mature source lock.

The source reported correlation does not provide the full 27-state vector. The course explicitly computes seven-sheet differences and retains source references for the larger result. Students may accurately cite the source report while acknowledging the narrower fresh check. Do not fill absent state entries with zeros: zero means observed absence under a known assignment, not unavailable data.

For the response singularity, use the repaired closure-consistent triple, not three identical 2π/3 phases. The example is a symbolic domain card. It is not a claimed direction in the frozen historical response geometry. For u=0 or F=0, retain inputs and flags; do not suppress the row or replace undefined values by zero.

The hard-mode prediction exercise uses four synthetic equally exposed states so students can compute the contrast. A real source-aligned test requires a frozen 27-state mapping and survey selection contract. This is a concrete limitation of the demonstration. Encourage competing mechanisms that explain recurrence, anisotropy and mismatches together; require each to state which new observable separates it from alternatives.

## Assessment and recovery

Use three capstone periods, with 32 available points in the eight-row rubric. The full key gives reconstruction and evidence boundaries, not only numerical outputs. Give targeted feedback by the missing criterion: type/domain, operation, reconstruction/source class or retention/boundary. A student who gets a scalar correct but discards signs or claims a force calibration has a substantive repair to make.

Offer oral explanation, a labeled coordinate sketch or a written derivation as equivalent demonstrations of a concept, while requiring the same finite calculations. If most students fail a diagnostic, revisit the earliest prerequisite and add a recovery period; document the pacing change. Stronger students can explore alternative skew centers or contrast definitions, clearly labeled as extensions. Do not require general eigenvalue solving, advanced group theory or physical-distance decoding.

## Reproducibility and maintenance

Run verify_examples.py through the tf_gpu_env Conda command specified in input-contract.json. It checks pinned source hashes, synthetic fractions, seven-sheet decimal differences and compact matrix residuals. It does not copy catalogs or large arrays. The source-comparison Markdown is regenerated from the original small G01 record. A source hash mismatch is a new provenance branch, not permission to silently update teaching constants.

Manifest checkpoint hashes are the recovery authority. Before resuming, verify them; patch the earliest defective lesson, then rebuild the verbatim assembly and refresh affected hashes. audit_report.json records actual local, cumulative, independent and final checks. completion_report.json covers hashes of every deliverable and the manifest. The deterministic validator checks structural completion, not epistemic truth or classroom outcomes; no real student trial is claimed.
