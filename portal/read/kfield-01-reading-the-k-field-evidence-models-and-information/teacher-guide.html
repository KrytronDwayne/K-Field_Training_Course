Teacher guide

This short course teaches how to read a research claim and build a recoverable evidence map. It is designed for seniors with the arithmetic capabilities in course-map.md. All core calculations are finite. Do not turn source recognition into an unprepared linear-algebra examination: Lesson 5 teaches a diagonal analogue, while the full source identity is assessed through annotation.

Preparation and pacing

Supply lessons, glossary, and sources.md; learners need no network, database, or special software. A basic calculator is optional. The long clock-rate numeral is copied as reported metadata, not inferred from a classroom experiment. Preserve the distinction between source facts and invented examples. Teachers can cover worked answers until students attempt the tasks.

Pacing is a planning assumption, not a classroom-tested result. If Lesson 5 needs more time, add a 25-minute coordinate practice period before the capstone. Do not omit the origin, units, or source-scope annotations to fit the clock. Paired reading can reduce text load. Ask learners to explain signs and units aloud before using a calculator.

Diagnose and repair

In Lesson 1, listen for “the cause was measured.” Ask students to point to the actual recorded value and then state the proposed process separately. Accept multiple labels when a sentence contains multiple clauses, provided those clauses are separated. Definitions and derived implications are not empirical observations.

At the Unit A boundary after Lesson 2, require a full path, fingerprint, locator, role, and access limit for a claim. A source ID alone is shorthand, not a complete standalone address. Keep S06's 97-state replay separate from acquisition counts. No samples-per-channel convention follows just from dividing total samples by ten.

In Lesson 3, a sign error breaks reconstruction. Have learners add model and residual back at every row. If an average replaces a sequence, use the two zero-sum sequences to show missing order. A statistic can describe retained information but cannot replace all of it. All zeros and nonzero residuals remain in the ledger.

At the Unit B boundary after Lesson 4, require an explanation of why u = 1 fails to separate A and B, and both residuals for x = 5 at u = 3. If students revise a rule after seeing a reading, label the revision and retain the original prediction. Do not pretend the revised rule was the initial prediction.

In Lesson 5, check origin subtraction, the −4 scale, and the nonzero-scale condition. Ask why 11 mm differs from 11 DU. The full source B is not the synthetic diagonal B. Source closure discrepancy remains source-reported; computing a classroom analogue does not recheck the full archived basis.

Capstone use

Use two 55-minute periods. First: 10 minutes reviewing addresses, 35 minutes drafting evidence maps, 10 minutes peer questions. Second: 25 minutes calculations and predictions, 20 minutes revision, 10 minutes defense. Assign only the student section of capstone.md; retain its teacher key and rubric for grading. Learners may use lessons and sources.md. No external experiment or invented reconstruction of missing raw records is required.

Award points for clear assumptions, accurate source scope, complete signed residuals, and reversible rules. A score alone does not repair a lost residual or evidence promotion: require corrected work alongside the original attempt. Novel mechanisms are admissible when explicitly proposed, internally consistent and testable under a declared comparison.

Source and recovery boundaries

All five supplied archival hashes were verified. Selected S02 fields and the exact bounded S06 narrative were inspected. This course does not claim fresh proof compilation, full locked-matrix verification, or raw instrument analysis. S06's unavailable raw residual is an explicit access limit, not a student failure. Synthetic tables allow complete reconstruction practice without manufacturing empirical data.

The manifest and completion report supply file hashes; course.md assembles lesson text verbatim. verify_examples.py and verification.json support arithmetic replay. The deterministic validator checks integrity and assembly; the educational audit checks meaning, prerequisites, and assessment alignment. Each has a separate scope.
