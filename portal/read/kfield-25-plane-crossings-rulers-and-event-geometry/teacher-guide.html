Teacher guide

Teach the course as a sequence of reconstructible calculations, not a list of formulas. Give students paper coordinate diagrams, fraction arithmetic and the supplied input cards. A calculator is optional; no Python, celestial software or calculus is required. All student examples and observations in this course are labeled synthetic. The source files remain in place and are accessed only for provenance or instructor context.

Entry check and repair

Before Lesson 1 ask students to evaluate (3/5)(−1), solve 2t=1, subtract −1 from 3, and read a point (2,0,0). Answers: −3/5, t=1/2, 4, and a point two units along the first axis. Repair signed arithmetic with number-line moves and the meaning of an ordered triple. Do not require a recalled matrix formula from earlier courses. A unit normal and dot product are constructed in Lesson 1 itself.

Pacing with actual tasks

Every period is 55 minutes: 5 retrieval, 20 teaching/worked example, 15 practice, 15 diagnostic/repair. Use diagnostics orally before showing their worked answers; ask students to explain why each sign or missing-input label matters.

If a gate fails, add a recovery period with the corresponding answered practice using numbers unchanged until the operation is understood. Record the added period and dependent delay in the teaching log; do not simplify away the zero, negative or gap cases. The eleven-period core is an estimate supported by the split sessions, not a claim that every learner needs identical time.

Questions that reveal understanding

Ask “Which input changed?” before a recalculation. Ask students to point to the stored signed rate after they compute its magnitude. At L5 compare a straight-line passage, a square contact and a jump using a small table of values. At L6 ask them to name the two equations producing a coincidence. At L7 exchange ledgers and recompute two endpoint pairs without asking the author for missing assumptions.

Common repairs: A negative signed distance is valid; normalize the normal before using it as distance. A moving origin needs relative displacement. A zero rate gives no finite traversal period, and might mean occupancy. Period and wavelength are different units. A sign change needs continuity for a zero-existence conclusion. A measured timing bound, an exact-model root and a residual difference are different fields. Polarity is declared independently from travel direction.

Assessment and differentiation

Use the 28-point capstone rubric as written. All seven steps have explicit prior explanations and answered practice. A correct number with the wrong source/evidence label loses the classification point. Preserve first attempts with the correction; a mistaken result is useful evidence of what bridge is missing. Require repair of omitted geometry, fabricated gap roots and lost signs regardless of total score.

For support, use axis-aligned normals before (3/5,4/5,0), and turn formulas into component arithmetic. For extension, students may derive the moving-family derivative after learning calculus, or compare a fully supplied real trajectory. These are optional and not hidden in the required assessment. Source-only matrix, gauge and compiler notation is instructor context, not vocabulary students must manipulate.

Instructor source and evidence guard

The course freshly verifies local file identities and synthetic calculations, not the entire K-Field proof corpus or its compiler. T10/T45 were read as pinned bounded artifacts. Source-notes.md records precise pointers and an underlying-contract retrieval limitation. Do not replace a missing proof body with a claim of proof failure or fresh proof success. The pinned T45 statement about a finite displayed alignment candidate set is narrower than a universal alignment conclusion; no such conclusion is assessed here.

No real acquisition is copied into this course. If teaching with later real observations, keep originals in place, add immutable references, preserve every sample/residual and state the response/calibration contract before drawing physical conclusions. The course's artificial grids are never substitutes for source-locked Cell3 or galaxy-derived cells.

Assembly and maintenance

course.md contains the exact seven lessons in order and links to the glossary and capstone. Any correction starts in the lesson file, propagates to the capstone/key if needed, then triggers a new assembly and refreshed manifest hashes. Run the project-root validator after repairs. See audit_report.json for actual checks and independent-audit.json for external-to-author content review; a machine validator alone is not a teaching judgment.
