Teaching pathways through the K-Field series

Use this guide with START-HERE, which shows current course acceptance. These are curriculum paths, not shortened versions of the courses. Each path includes every prerequisite listed in the series ledger. A course still in production is not ready to teach.

Each course is a topical unit with several lessons. A lesson may require more than one 55-minute period. Use the completed course teacher guide for pacing; early blueprint durations are planning estimates. The full sequence is a multi-term curriculum, not a promised one-year timetable.

Teach an individual course

Begin with the entry skills in its teacher guide. Use the listed prerequisite courses, or check that students can already perform their required operations; a familiar topic name alone is not a mastery check. Retain all prerequisite explanations and practice needed for the chosen capstone.

Teach from the individual lesson files or the identical assembled course. Have students attempt each practice before revealing its worked answer. Use the diagnostic questions to locate the earliest missing skill, then repair that operation and retain both the original and corrected work. The capstone combines previously taught operations; its rubric specifies how to award method credit and which material errors require repair.

The course folder contains the student lessons, glossary, teacher guide, capstone with full key and rubric, source notes, and verification records. Give students the task portion of the capstone before the answer key. Source and audit files let instructors trace conventions and scope; students are not expected to run compilers or reconstruct unavailable archival bodies.

Keep the meaning of a result explicit

Ask students to state the input, operation, output, retained information and claim scope. Separate observed records, derived consequences, inferred structure, proposed mechanisms and reference-theory comparisons. A source-reported theorem keeps its stated premises and provenance; a small fresh calculation verifies the operation actually performed. Synthetic numbers are worked examples, not measurements.

Coordinates, metrics, parity labels and module numbers have local meanings. Preserve basis order, units, origins and sign conventions when crossing courses. In particular, compiler M labels and mathematical-backbone M labels are different namespaces; physical velocities and transformed phase vectors are different quantities. The shared rules in the series map and each course glossary identify these distinctions.

Keep raw observations, residual coordinates, failed synthetic trials and reconstruction information. A reduced description must retain what is needed to recover its declared input. Missing archival inputs stay visible as limitations; an authored example is never substituted for a missing source certificate.

Assess the series

Use each course capstone before advancing to dependent operations. Keep an assessment portfolio with the original solution, correction, source card and explanation of the result’s limits. Course 30 brings the branches together in a research design and source-addressed dependency argument; planning an experiment does not claim that the experiment has been performed.

The accepted course reports document content review and finite verification. Pacing remains an instructional allocation until tested with learners. Extend practice time when needed rather than omitting prerequisite work.
