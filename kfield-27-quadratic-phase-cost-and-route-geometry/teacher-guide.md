# Teacher guide

Teach the seven lessons in order over 13 core periods of 55 minutes, then the three-period capstone. Students need paper, coordinate axes and basic arithmetic; a calculator is optional for plotting decimal approximations. Keep exact fractions in proofs. Read sources.md before discussing the source corpus: the course has a complete synthetic calculation chain and a deliberately explicit source-certificate retrieval boundary.

## Entry check and recovery

Ask students to calculate 2(−1)²+2(−1)(2)+2(2)²=6, add (1,1)+(1,−1)=(2,0), divide 6 by 3, and plot (1,−1). Ask why 2·(1/2)=1 and (1/√2)²=1/2. If needed, rehearse distributivity, signs and squares before Lesson 1's matrix shorthand. This is a prerequisite check, not a claim about completion of the four preceding series courses.

Each period has 5 minutes retrieval, 20 explanation/worked example, 15 guided practice and 15 independent diagnostics/repair. For two-period lessons, use the first session for the first named focus in course-map.md, the second for the remaining focus and independent reproduction. Students should attempt P1 before viewing its answers. Ask them to articulate why the answer has its units, not merely imitate a number.

In Lessons 2, 3 and 5, reserve the second session's 15-minute practice block for P1a: construct the ruler matrix, multiply the forward pullback, and reproduce the general finite bound proof respectively. Use the first session's practice block for the numerical P1 application. These explicit independent tasks close the suboperation-level capstone prerequisites.

## Unit-boundary checks

After Lesson 3, ask students to explain why G_ruler, classroom G, G_phase and M_cell are not interchangeable; verify both direction and inverse coordinate maps. After Lesson 5, have students produce a complete shared-specification ledger and finite minimum proof from blank paper. After Lesson 7, ask them to separate a synthetic proof, source-reported claim, retrieved missing-body status and proposed detector transfer. These cumulative checks test actual prerequisites for the capstone. Add a recovery period if students cannot independently reproduce the necessary operation; record that scheduling adjustment rather than hiding it by speeding up the next lesson.

## Capstone administration and scoring

Period 1: complete the typed input/source ledger and Tasks 1–3. Period 2: Tasks 4–5 and their reconstructed paths. Period 3: Tasks 6–7, source classifications and repairs. The key has seven four-point rows, totaling 28. Return specific item-level feedback. A total score alone must not conceal an omitted cross term, missing duration or fabricated source value. Require correction of each such conceptual defect before marking that operation mastered. Retain the student's original work alongside corrections so the change is visible.

All required operations were introduced and practiced. Do not assess continuum integration, generalized eigenvalues, real ICRS trajectories, unknown source matrices or radiometric conversion. The source audit's correct answer includes missing fields; students are not expected to discover an unavailable certificate. Accept accurate alternate finite calculations with the same units and declared rules.

## Evidence and residual practice

Do not turn source reported status into a course-verified measurement. Do not silently lower or strengthen the source's own attributed claim either. Cost symmetry under reversal does not erase velocity signs. Average/deviation representation must retain every δ_i and duration. With a detector model, keep readings, predictions, calibration, residual signs, times and relevant conditions; a residual invites further decoding. No exclusion or statistical threshold is needed for these finite examples.

There is no claim here that phase cost is force, transferred energy or a detector law. Invite mechanism proposals, but ask which explicit mapping and observations would make them sufficient to explain the response. Separate that constructive inquiry from the already completed algebraic route proof.

## Reproducibility and recovery

Student text is in lesson-01.md through lesson-07.md; course.md assembles those lessons verbatim in manifest order. capstone.md includes full answers and rubric. verify_examples.py independently computes rational examples and writes compact verification.json; no source data is copied. manifest.json identifies checkpoints and hashes; completion_report.json hashes the manifest and non-self deliverables. independent-audit.md and audit_report.json record review and dispositions. The project-root validator checks content identity, paths, statuses, hashes and audit consistency; it complements human teaching review rather than replacing it.
